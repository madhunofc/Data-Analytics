Does the pressure definition separate the moments that matter?

Every ball gets a Leverage Index: how much the next ball is expected to swing the batting side's win probability, divided by the average swing. It is multiplied by 1.25 in knockouts, by 1.2 for batters after 2+ wickets in the last 3 overs, and by 1.2 for bowlers who conceded 30+ in the last 3 overs. The top 25% of balls are pressure balls.

Method, definitions and limitations

1. Data preparation

The two ball-by-ball files are one table split mid-match (part 2 has no header). Super-over balls, rain-reduced (DLS) matches and no-result matches are removed. Extras runs are mostly missing in the source, so each wide or no-ball counts as 1 run and each bye or leg-bye as 1 run. Chase targets are then made consistent with the recorded winner (— innings adjusted).

2. Pressure (hybrid: model plus flags)

  • Two gradient-boosted win-probability models with monotonic constraints. The 1st-innings model uses score, wickets, balls and run rate. The chase model uses runs needed, balls left, wickets in hand, required rate and target. Both include an IPL flag.
  • Leverage Index = Σ P(outcome) × |WP after outcome − WP now| over dot, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, wicket and wide, using outcome rates by innings and phase. It is divided by its mean so that 1.0 is an average ball.
  • Flags: knockout match ×1.25. Batting collapse (2+ wickets in the last 18 legal balls) ×1.2 for batters. Bowler under attack (30+ runs in the last 18 legal balls) ×1.2 for bowlers.
  • The top 25% of balls by this score are pressure balls. Sensitivity checks re-run the ranking at 20% and 30%.

3. Performance (B3 baseline)

  • Expected runs and dismissal rate per ball by innings × over × wickets band × pace/spin × competition, smoothed toward the phase average.
  • Wicket cost comes from a DLS-style run-expectancy curve fitted to this data: expected remaining runs = aw(1 − e−cw·balls left).
  • Run value per ball. Batter: (runs − expected) − wicket cost × (out − expected out). Bowler: the mirror image, with only bowler-credited wickets.
  • Win-probability added per ball is also credited to the striker and, with the sign reversed, to the bowler.

4. Rating

  • Eligibility: batters with 300+ balls faced and 100+ under pressure; bowlers with 360+ legal balls and 120+ under pressure.
  • Empirical-Bayes shrinkage of each per-ball rate toward the eligible-player mean, with prior strength k from method of moments. Batter pressure run value k ≈ — balls; bowler k ≈ — balls. Where method of moments found no detectable spread (bowler WPA, consistency), k is capped (1,500 balls; 30 games) so the term stays a tie-breaker.
  • Consistency: share of matches with 6+ pressure balls where the player's pressure run value was positive, shrunk with a beta-binomial prior.
  • Score = 0.40 × impact (mean of the z-scores of pressure run value and pressure WPA) + 0.25 × z(change vs own normal) + 0.20 × z(consistency) + 0.15 × z(log pressure balls).
  • Rank stability: 300 Poisson bootstrap resamples of matches, with the full rating recomputed each time.

5. Limitations

  • Missing extras make targets approximate by a few runs, which mainly affects leverage in the last overs of tight chases.
  • The 1st-innings win model is weak (AUC —), as expected, so 1st-innings leverage is flatter and most pressure balls fall in chases.
  • No adjustment for opposition strength. T20Is include associate nations.
  • Batting ranks below the top 3 overlap heavily in the resamples, and IPL and international pressure batting barely correlate. Treat ranks 4–10 as a group, not a strict order.
