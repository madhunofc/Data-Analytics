What changed from Sample 1

Pressure is continuousEvery ball carries its pressure level (standardised log leverage, with the knockout, collapse and under-attack multipliers). Nothing depends on a top-25% cut-off.
Opponent is adjustedBatter and bowler effects are fitted together on each ball, so runs against weak attacks count for less and wickets of strong batters count for more.
One model, tuned by dataShrinkage strength for levels and pressure slopes is picked by 3-fold cross-validation grouped by match. Sample 1 used a hand-weighted composite.
Uncertainty built in100 bootstrap refits give a 90% interval for each rating and rank, and split-half tests measure how well ratings repeat.

Method, model equation and limitations

Shared with Sample 1

Same data cleaning, calibrated win-probability models, Leverage Index (with ×1.25 knockout, ×1.2 collapse and ×1.2 under-attack flags), context-expected runs and wickets, and DLS-style wicket cost. That gives each ball a run value for the batter and for the bowler.

Model (fitted separately for batting and bowling)

RV_ball = μ + γ·z + a[player] + b[player]·z + a[opponent] + b[opponent]·z + ε z = (log pressure score − mean) / sd, clipped to ±3 penalty = λ·Σ(a²) + λ·r·Σ(b²) (ridge ≙ Gaussian random effects)
  • Clutch rating = a + b·z90, the expected run value on a 90th-percentile pressure ball against an average opponent. Rankings use this.
  • Normal = a + b·z50. Lift = b·(z90 − z50), the player's own change under pressure (the B3 own-baseline idea, now opponent-adjusted).
  • λ ∈ {30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000} and r ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64} are chosen by 3-fold cross-validation grouped by match, on held-out squared error.
  • All players enter the model, so the opponent terms are complete. Only players meeting Sample 1's eligibility (bat 300+ balls and 100+ top-25% pressure balls; bowl 360+ and 120+) are ranked.
  • Bootstrap: 100 Poisson-weighted refits by match. Split-half: 6 random match splits, Pearson r scaled up with Spearman–Brown.

Limitations

  • Cross-validation shrinks the pressure slopes hard, because individual pressure effects are small next to ball-level noise. Most lift intervals cross 0, so the clutch rating mostly reflects how good a player is (opponent-adjusted) in the situations that matter, with only a small player-specific pressure adjustment. Sample 1's delta term rewards pressure-specific change more strongly but is far less repeatable.
  • Big-hitting players with short careers (for example under 1,000 balls) can rank high on level alone. Check their wide intervals before relying on them.
  • Opponent adjustment covers the batter–bowler pairing only, not fielding or venue.
  • The extras-runs gap in the source data affects targets and leverage in the same way as in Sample 1.
